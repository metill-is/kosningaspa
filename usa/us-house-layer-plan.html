Plan: a House district layer for the US model

3 November 2026 (election day). Branch claude/usa-midterms-model-feasibility-0897e9, kept local.

Treat each of the 435 districts as a race in the existing race layer: national margin + district lean + incumbency + race error. Seat counts, P(Democrats control the House) and P(both chambers) then come out of the same draws as the Senate. The results history is already in hand (Cha–Kuriwaki–Snyder, CC0, every House race 2006–2024 with incumbency). The one hard input is district lean on each map, the presidential vote by congressional district, which has no open ready-made source and must be built from precinct results. The model is tested on 2018–2024. The 2026 House results arrive tonight, before the model exists, so 2026 can only be a reported check, never a clean test.

Model sketch

The Senate race layer already gives each race a deviation from the national walk, a regression prior learnt from past results and a shared poll error per race. Districts slot into it with House-specific covariates and their own race-type terms:

margind = national + βlean·leand + βinc·incd + βprev·prevd·same_mapd + ηHouse + δd
polld,i = margind + biaspollster + κd + noise
  • lean: the last presidential margin on the district's current lines, minus the national presidential margin.
  • inc: Democratic incumbent, open seat or Republican incumbent. Freshmen get their own level, since the sophomore surge is real.
  • prev: the last House margin, only where the lines did not change and the race was contested. Its weight is learnt.
  • ηHouse: an error shared by every House race in a year, the House counterpart of the Senate's sη (review step 4). Without it the 435 districts act as independent coin flips and the seat interval comes out far too narrow.
  • Uncontested seats go to the party running, outside the likelihood. California and Washington top-two races between two candidates of one party do the same.
  • Cost: δ is integrated out and drawn in generated quantities, as for the Senate, so 435 races add little sampling cost. The history adds about 3,800 contested results; a simulated 470-race layer fitted in 24 min earlier in the study.

Decisions

All four accepted as recommended, 3 November 2026. Step 1 then found VEST is not open, so the open source for decision 1 is MEDSL's precinct returns (CC0).

1. Where can district lean come from? Open-only means building it from precinct data (step 3), which is slow but publishable. The Downballot's tables would be a day's work but have no stated licence. My recommendation: build it openly and use the Downballot only to check our figures, never as an input.

2. What counts as the test? The 2026 results will be public before v13 exists, so they cannot test it cleanly. My recommendation: 2018–2024 is the test, 2026 is reported as a check, and nothing is tuned after the certified 2026 results are opened. Commit v13 before December so the order can be proved from git.

3. How far back? 2018–2024 needs four map vintages. Adding 2008–2016 would need the 2002 maps and more precinct data, giving more cycles for ηHouse at roughly twice the data work. My recommendation: start with 2018–2024 and extend only if ηHouse is poorly identified from three cycles.

4. What is the success bar? Proposal: across the three backtests, House seat intervals cover 3 of 3 results, the district Brier score beats uniform swing, and the 90% intervals for contested districts cover 85–95% of them.

Risks

  • Precinct matching. Precincts are renamed, split and merged between years, so the name match across a map change will miss some. The share of votes matched is reported per state, and a state below about 95% gets a wider lean error or falls back to the state's own statistics for the new map. Step 3's check measures it.
  • 2026 maps still in court. If a map was blocked or replaced close to the election, the lean comes from the map that was actually used, and step 1 has to record which one that was.
  • Three test cycles. ηHouse is learnt from very few years. Its prior should come from the House poll misses in 538's raw_polls, as sη did for the Senate.
  • Public repository. The precinct data and derived lean tables stay in the study folder, as the poll tables do. Only code and summary scores go into git.
