The plain picture

Every model in kosningaspá measures pollster bias against election results. A result enters the likelihood with no bias term, so it fixes the truth scale; a poll's bias is how far its expected shares sit from the latent support on its date. What differs between the models is whether that bias may change over time.

  • The forecast model (production) has one bias for the whole window from 1 Jan 2016. Nothing updates it at an election; each new result only adds evidence about the same constant. On election day it puts part of the bias back (the hedge), so the forecast corrects about three quarters of the logit-scale bias it learnt.
  • Fylgisvakt v4 (on metill.is until 5 Nov 2026) also has one bias for its whole window and removes it in full.
  • Fylgisvakt v5 (on master since 29 Oct 2026, on metill.is since 5 Nov 2026) has one bias per epoch, the stretch between two results. The bias is constant inside an epoch and takes one random-walk step at each result.
  • The v2 forecast (harness only, not yet used for a forecast) had v5's epoch bias as its step M1 from 6 Nov 2026 (PR #21) until 10 Nov 2026, when it was removed; it now keeps one constant bias with no hedge, and lets the walk move faster on the step leaving an election.

So, in v5, the bias changes at elections and never between them. The catch is the open epoch since the last result. Nothing pins it at its far end, so its bias is read off the first post-election polls against the result behind them. For D, that reading has sat closer to zero than the next election's poll error in each of the last three cycles (section Worked example).

What “bias” means in these models

Every row of data, poll or result, gets a linear predictor on the log-ratio scale. In v4:

$$\eta_n \;=\; \beta_{t(n)} \;+\; \gamma_{h(n)}, \qquad \gamma_h \;=\; \mu \;+\; d_h, \qquad \gamma_{\text{election}} = 0 .$$

A poll is Dirichlet-multinomial on $\operatorname{softmax}(\eta_n)$ over the parties it reports, with its house's precision $\varphi_h$. A result is house 1: $\gamma = 0$, a plain multinomial, no $\varphi$. So $\beta$ lives on the result scale, and $\gamma_h$ is the house's poll-minus-truth gap in log-ratio terms. It splits into the industry bias $\mu$, shared by every house, and the house's deviation $d_h$. v5 indexes both by epoch: $\gamma_{e,h} = \mu_e + d_{e,h}$. In v2, $\mu_e$ changes only at a newcomer's entry and $d_h$ is constant.

Zero sum

In v4, v5 and v2, $\mu$ and every $d_h$ are sum_to_zero_vector[P] over all ten parties. Only contrasts between parties are identified: a poll's softmax is unchanged by adding a constant, and it speaks only to the parties it reports. Two consequences.

  • 0 on the logit scale is the unweighted mean of the ten parties, not “no bias”. The sign does not say whether polls over- or understate a party: in the 29 Oct fit, 17 house cells change sign between the logit scale and percentage points (commit 6d884d9, merged in 851965e).
  • With std_normal() on a sum_to_zero_vector, Stan's isometric transform gives each element a marginal sd of $\sqrt{1-1/P} = 0.949$, not 1.
Percentage points

Every pp figure in this chapter is measured per poll and per posterior draw. For a poll on date $t$ reporting parties $C$, the bias's effect is

$$\Delta \;=\; \operatorname{softmax}\!\big(\log \pi_t[C] + b[C]\big) \;-\; \pi_t[C]\big/\textstyle\sum \pi_t[C],$$

where $\pi_t$ is the latent support on that date. A per-epoch figure averages $\Delta$ over that epoch's polls (computed by the comparison script of the 29 Oct v5 review, which is not in the repository). Negative means the polls understate the party. As a rule of thumb, a logit shift $b$ of one party alone moves it, at share $p$, by about $p(1-p)\,b$: at 20%, 0.1 logit is about 1.6 pp.

The forecast model codes the constraint differently. Its linear predictor drops one party: $\eta_1 = -\sum_{p\ge 2}\eta_p$, with Annað as party 1. House effects are free for eight parties (D, B, S, V, P, C, F, M), and the ninth, Sósíalistar (J), closes each house's column as minus their sum. So Annað's poll log-odds carry no house term at all, and J's bias is implied rather than estimated. That is $P-2 = 8$ free dimensions where a softmax identifies $P-1 = 9$: a real restriction, not just a normalisation. Fylgisvakt had the same drop-one form until v4 replaced it on 24 Jun 2026 (commit 6d93978). The reason was reference dependence: with matched seeds, the old model gave P(D > S) 0.85 with Samfylkingin as the reference and 0.48 with Annað.

What tells a model the bias: the election anchors

Each result pins the latent walk on its date. In v5 the 2024 result is 212,471 votes in a multinomial with no overdispersion; in the 29 Oct fit, the latent support on 30 Nov 2024 sits within 0.02 pp of the result for every party. Since the anchored-walk reparameterisation (aa2ac1a for the forecast model, 28 Oct 2026; fdf7590 for v4 and v5, merged on 29 Oct in 723b533), the forecast model, v4 and v5 all build the walk from those anchor levels, with Brownian bridges between them. The prior on the path is unchanged. The same commits cap $\log\varphi$ at 20, which conditions the posterior on $\log\varphi \lt 20$.

Between anchors the model sees only $\beta + \gamma$. The bias stays identified because the walk is continuous and slow. Polls just after a result and polls just before the next one are tied to those anchors through the walk, so they carry nearly all the information about the bias. Polls in mid-term mostly tell the model about $\beta$. One consequence matters later: anything systematic between the last polls and the vote, such as late swing or differential turnout, is absorbed into the bias. That point is from the author's industry-bias dossier of 9 Oct 2026 (private project notes).

  • A closed epoch (E2–E4) is pinned at both ends: polls just after its opening result and just before its closing one both measure its constant bias.
  • The open epoch (E5) has only its opening anchor. Its bias is learnt from how the early post-election polls sat against that result, shrunk towards the previous epoch's bias by $\tau_\mu$. Poll bias and genuine post-election movement are told apart only by $\tau$ against the walk's volatility $\sigma$, as the v5 source's header states.
  • E1 on the 2016 window has no opening anchor: the first poll (16 Jan 2016) predates the first result (29 Oct 2016). Before that anchor the walk runs backward from it, with only $\beta_1 \sim N(0, 2)$ at the first date.

The forecast model: one bias, and a hedge on election day

Stan/polling_and_fundamentals_kjordaemi.stan builds each house's effect non-centred, for the eight free parties:

gamma[p, 2:H] = mu_gamma[p] + sigma_gamma[p] * gamma_raw[p, ];   // p = D, B, S, V, P, C, F, M
gamma[P - 1, h] = -sum(gamma[1:(P - 2), h]);                     // J closes each house
// house 1 (the election) is 0 for every party; Annað (party 1) has no house term

Stan/polling_and_fundamentals_kjordaemi.stan, transformed parameters (master); the same block in 9381677.

  • Priors: gamma_raw ~ N(0,1), mu_gamma ~ N(0,1) and sigma_gamma ~ Exponential(1), each per party. J's implied industry prior has sd $\sqrt 8 \approx 2.83$, against 1 for the others.
  • No time index. gamma is matrix[P-1, H], so one value per house and party spans 2016-01-01 to election day. The anchors in the 2024 forecast's window are the 2016, 2017 and 2021 results. The 2021–24 polls have no closing result and read the same $\gamma$. The walk's end on election day is the fundamentals prior, $\beta_T = \mu_{\text{pred}} + \tau_f\, z \odot \sigma$.
  • Kjördæmi polls (from 2021-09-25) carry the house's national effect gamma[, house] plus the constituency effect $\delta$. There is no kjördæmi-specific house effect.

The hedge

mu_gamma_weight = beta_rng(40, 60);
mu_gamma_pred[1:(P - 2)] = mu_gamma;
mu_gamma_pred[P - 1] = - sum(mu_gamma);
mu_gamma_pred[1:(P - 3)] /= sqrt(3.0);    // D, B, S, V, P, C, F
mu_gamma_pred[P - 2] /= sqrt(2.0);        // M
mu_gamma_pred *= mu_gamma_weight;         // added to beta on the forecast days only

Generated quantities (master; identical in 9381677).

$\beta$ is the fully corrected state. In generated quantities only, the forecast adds back $w\,\mu_p/\sqrt{n_e}$, with a fresh $w \sim$ Beta(40, 60) each draw (mean 0.40, 90% 0.32–0.48); the data never inform $w$. The divisors match $\sqrt{n_e}$, the number of anchor elections since 2016 the party ran in: 3 for D, B, S, V, P, C and F, 2 for M; J's row is not divided, as for $n_e = 1$. The bias left in is then $0.4/\sqrt{n_e}$ on average, a logit-scale correction of about 77%, 72% and 60%. The block entered git in the clean-up commit 9381677 (25 Feb 2026) and is unchanged on master. No comment, commit or document records why Beta(40, 60), √3 or √2 were chosen. The v2 design drops the hedge (section v2).

Only the forecast reads the hedge: the national series after the last fitted day and the kjördæmi forecast. The in-sample replications leave it out, as the likelihood does: y_rep_national up to day $D$ draws from $\beta$ alone, and y_rep_k from $\beta + \gamma + \delta$. Until PR #15 (commits f51c3c6 and 253d037, merged 4 Nov 2026) both replications added it, which put the hedge into posterior predictive checks of data the model had fitted without it.

Known issues
  • Annað has no house effect. A bias in how the polls measure Annað against the other parties cannot be corrected, and J's bias is only implied. This bites when a sizeable party is folded into Annað.
  • The hedge breaks the zero sum. After the √3/√2 scaling, mu_gamma_pred no longer sums to zero, so Annað's predicted share moves too.
  • Master's divisors fit 2024 only. The /√3 and /√2 rows match only the 2024 party set. The harness's R0 arm instead passes mu_gamma_scale $= 1/\sqrt{n_e}$ as data, set to 0 when $n_e = 0$.
  • Old data files keep the wrong anchor totals. get_election_data() used to recycle three vote totals across its 30 rows, which moved anchor shares by up to 0.36 pp (M 2017). Since PR #15 it takes each election's valid votes by date. A data/polling_data.csv written before that keeps the recycled totals until R/correct_election_anchors.R rewrites them; the live file had them on 17 of its 30 Kosning rows (counted by running the script on a copy) until the author ran the script on it on 4 Nov 2026. The first refit after that moves the 2016, 2017 and 2021 anchors by up to that 0.36 pp.

Fylgisvakt v4: one bias, removed in full

Stan/polling_watch_v4.stan: $\gamma_h = \mu + \sigma_\gamma\, f_h$ for each polling house, $\gamma_1 = 0$, with $\mu \sim N(0, I)$, $f_h \sim N(0, I)$ (all sum_to_zero_vector[P]) and one scalar $\sigma_\gamma \sim$ Exponential(1). Nothing depends on the date. The published curve is $\pi_t = \operatorname{softmax}(\beta_t)$, the latent support with the polls fully corrected; there is no hedge.

The v4 fit that metill.is showed until 5 Nov 2026 read a window starting at the 2021 result. That filter dates from 7e0c032 and was removed in fdf7590, because the old forward-walk parameterisation could not sample several anchors. So that fit's bias was learnt from 2021–24 polls between two anchors, plus post-2024 polls read against the 2024 result, and applied unchanged to the latest polls.

Fylgisvakt v5: a bias per epoch

Stan/polling_watch_v5_epoch.stan cuts the window at its results. Each stretch that holds polls is an epoch, and both the industry bias and each house's deviation take a random-walk step from one epoch to the next ($k$ indexes houses 2..H):

$$\begin{aligned} \mu_1 &= \mu_0, & \mu_e &= \mu_{e-1} + \tau_\mu\, z^{\mu}_{e-1},\\ d_{1,k} &= \sigma_\gamma\, f_k, & d_{e,k} &= d_{e-1,k} + \tau_{\text{house}}\, z^{h}_{e-1,k} \ \text{ for } e > f_k \text{ (else } d_{e-1,k}\text{)},\\ \gamma_{e,h} &= \mu_e + d_{e,h-1}, & \gamma_{e,1} &= 0 , \end{aligned}$$

where $f_k$ is house $k$'s first epoch. At a party's entry epoch the party restarts, as described in the list below.

  • A house's walk starts at its first poll (since 5 Nov 2026). Until its first epoch $f_k$, the epoch of its first poll, a house keeps its epoch-1 deviation $\sigma_\gamma f_k$, so a house that starts late carries no prior-only steps into its first polls. Before this change, Maskína (first poll in E3) entered with two such steps and Prósent (E2) with one, which widened their first deviations and let $\tau_{\text{house}}$ learn from them.
  • A newcomer restarts at its entry epoch (since 5 Nov 2026). A party's entry epoch is the first epoch in which a poll reports it: M in E2 and J in E3 on this window. No poll speaks to its bias before then. Because every bias vector sums to zero, the drift shared by the parties already polled is flat in the data too. So at entry, after that epoch's step, its industry bias and each walking house's deviation are reset to its epoch-1 value plus the mean drift of the parties that entered earlier: $\mu_e[p] = \mu_0[p] + \overline{(\mu_e - \mu_0)}_B$ and $d_{e,k}[p] = d_{1,k}[p] + \overline{(d_{e,k} - d_{1,k})}_B$, with $B$ the earlier parties. Each vector is then re-centred. The newcomer's contrasts with $B$ are those of its epoch-1 draw, and it walks with the rest afterwards. The v2 forecast's epoch bias adopted this rule for its house deviations in its fix F1 (cbc53d3, PR #21), before it was removed. With both $\tau$ at 0 nothing drifts, so v5 is still v4. R/sampler_check/exact_checks_polling_watch.R checks both rules exactly; R/sampler_check/RESULTS.md has the fits before and after.
  • Priors. $\mu_0$, $f_k$ and every shock $z$ are std_normal() on sum_to_zero_vector[P], with $\sigma_\gamma \sim$ Exponential(1). With estimate_tau = 1, the production default, $\tau_\mu$ and $\tau_{\text{house}}$ each get an independent half-normal(0, 0.1) prior (prepare_polling_watch_epochs()). Fixing both at 0 gives back v4, which is the model's own implementation check.
  • Constant within an epoch. The likelihood reads gamma[epoch_n[n], house_n[n]] and has no date term.
  • Not scaled by epoch length. The one-year epoch 2016–17 and the four-year 2017–21 take the same step size.
  • Which epoch a poll is in. raw_epoch = 1 + findInterval(date, election_dates, left.open = TRUE), so a poll dated on an election day belongs to the epoch that election closes. An epoch exists only once it holds a poll; an empty epoch between two results is an error.
  • A house that skips an epoch after its first. Its deviation still walks, but that cell carries no likelihood term: it is interpolated, extrapolated, or prior-only. fit_polling_watch.R writes polling_watch_gamma.parquet only for cells that hold a poll. polling_watch_mu_gamma.parquet holds every epoch.
  • What gets published. The curve is still $\operatorname{softmax}(\beta)$. The platform export reads neither $\gamma$ nor $\mu$, and its poll dots are the raw shares.

Decision

v5 was built on 3 Oct 2026 (88b8663). The motivating suspicion was that voters moving from Framsóknarflokkurinn to Miðflokkurinn took the hard-to-poll electorate with them, which a bias held constant across elections cannot follow. The author chose it for production on 28 Oct 2026, as the variant best grounded in theory. It merged as PR #11 (723b533) on 29 Oct 2026, and metill.is has shown it since 5 Nov 2026, starting with the fit in data/2026-11-05/.

The evidence, leave-future-out against the official results (Stan/polling_watch_variants.md §10):

  • 2024: v5 CRPS 0.542, MAE 0.73, 7 of 10 covered; v4 on the same window 0.844, 1.01, 4 of 10; the configuration live until 5 Nov (v4 from the previous result) 0.770, 1.08, 5 of 10.
  • 2021: v5 CRPS 0.865, 10 of 10 covered; v4 on the same window 0.854, 8 of 10; that configuration 1.293.
  • The hedge: adding back $w\mu$ helps v4 in 2024, but v5 scores best at $w = 0$ in both years.

Stan/polling_watch_variants.md §10 calls two elections thin evidence.

What happens when a result arrives

Sequence
  1. The result enters as a new house-1 row and becomes an anchor: $\beta$ on that date is pinned.
  2. In the forecast model and v4, the bias's structure does not change. The new anchor makes the polls around it informative about the same constant, and its estimate moves accordingly.
  3. In v5, the epoch that was open becomes closed. Its bias is now pinned at both ends, and its estimate is revised, because the polls just before the result now speak to it directly. E4's bias for D, −3.3 pp, comes from both ends in this way.
  4. The first poll after the result opens a new epoch. Its industry bias is the previous epoch's plus one shock, with sd about $0.95\,\tau_\mu$ per party. Each house's deviation steps by $\tau_{\text{house}}$.
  5. Until the next result, the open epoch's bias is learnt from how the early post-election polls sit against the result. Later polls add little: their drift goes to the walk. A one-party linear-Gaussian stand-in for E5 puts 94% of the weight on the four polls in the first 48 days.
Between elections

No model moves the bias between elections. In v5 the bias is constant within an epoch. A slow drift in a house's lean is averaged into that constant, and the walk takes the rest. One example: Gallup's D readings, measured against the fitted latent support, went from −3.3 pp in 2022 to −1.1 pp in Oct–Nov 2024.

Worked example: D at the 2024 boundary

v5's bias for D in E4, the 2021–24 term pinned at both ends, is −3.30 pp. What moves is E5.

Why E5 moved

  • The open epoch rests on its first polls. In order, they were Maskína 16.3 (12 Dec), Gallup 20.1 (24 Dec), Maskína 19.3 (11 Jan) and Gallup 20.5 (17 Jan), against D's result of 19.4. The 12 December Maskína poll fits E4's bias. The other three sit at or above the result.
  • Keeping E4's biases would need a jump in the walk. With Gallup at −2.5 and Maskína at −4.1, latent D would have to reach 22.6% by day 24, 23.4% by day 42 and 23.0% by day 48. The fitted walk's one-sd change in D's share from the 2024 result is 0.91 pp over 24 days, 1.20 over 42 and 1.29 over 48. Those rises are moves of 2.8–3.5 sd.
  • A bias step is far cheaper. The industry shock E4→E5 for D is 0.128 logit, or 0.86 $\tau_\mu$, and P(shock > 0) = 0.98.
  • Both houses moved. Gallup went from −2.47 to +0.33 pp, Maskína from −4.12 to −1.37. So this is not the composition change of Prósent (−4.55 in E4) and Félagsvísindastofnun dropping out.
  • One nuance. Gallup's lean had been shrinking through E4 already. Its D polls minus v5's latent D, both over the parties the poll reports, averaged −3.3 pp in 2022, −2.5 in 2023 and −1.1 in Oct–Nov 2024, then +0.3 in E5. Measured from that late level, its step at the election is about 1.4 pp. Maskína's lean stayed between −3.2 and −5.2 by quarter through E4 (−3.75 in Oct–Nov 2024), then −1.0 in 2025 and −1.7 in 2026.

Reading

An open epoch measures the polls against the result behind them. That is a different quantity from the polls' error at the next election, and the model cannot tell them apart. On D's record so far, v5's −0.7 pp is likely too close to zero as a guide to the next election. When the next result arrives, E5 will be re-estimated from both ends.

The effect is visible now. On the polls to 27 Sep 2026, the configuration then live (v4 from the 2021 result, constant D bias, refitted to those polls) puts D at 26.6% and v5 at 24.8%, and v5 widens Samfylkingin's lead: P(S ahead) 0.86 against 0.80 (variants.md §10). The v4 fit on metill.is until 5 Nov (polls to 14 Sep) had D at 26.8% and P(S ahead) 0.82; the v5 fit that replaced it (data/2026-11-05/, polls to 15 Oct) has D at 24.3% and P(S ahead) 0.83. This reading is Claude's, from the data above. It has not been tested by a backtest.

The priors on the bias walk, and how loose they are

  • Prior. $\tau_\mu, \tau_{\text{house}} \sim$ half-normal(0, 0.1) on the logit scale: median 0.067, 90% quantile 0.164, 95% quantile 0.196.
  • Posterior (29 Oct fit). $\tau_\mu$ = 0.147 sits at the prior's 86th percentile, so the data pull it up against the prior: the prior is not what makes the walk loose. $\tau_{\text{house}}$ = 0.050 sits below the prior median: houses tend to move together, and the industry level carries most of the change.
  • In pp. A zero-sum shock moves every party, and the others' moves feed back through the softmax. So one sd of an industry step moves party $p$ by about $\tau_\mu\, \pi_p \lVert e_p - \pi \rVert$, more than the $0.95\,\tau_\mu\, \pi_p(1-\pi_p)$ its own element alone would give. For D that is about 2.5 pp at its 2024 share and 3.0 pp at 25%. D's E4→E5 move of 2.6 pp is about one sd; its logit shock, 0.86 $\tau_\mu$, is just under one (one sd is $0.95\,\tau_\mu$). For comparison, the v2 prior-predictive check gives an industry step for a 20% party of −3.0 to +2.9 pp (5–95%), from the prior alone.

One scale for every party

$\tau_\mu$ is a single scalar on the logit scale, shared by all ten parties and all four epoch boundaries. For a small party, a pp-sized poll error is a large logit step: V had 2.3% in 2024, P 3.0% and Annað 1.1%. Their shocks therefore set the scale.

The v2 forecast: the epoch bias, built and removed

The epoch bias was built into the v2 forecast as its step M1 and merged to master with PR #21 (merge commit 2bc25f0, 6 Nov 2026), as the epoch_bias switch of Stan/forecast_v2.stan: v5's epoch walk of the industry bias and the house deviations, with Annað's own step scale, a newcomer restart at its entry epoch and no hedge. On the two cells where both were scored, the model with the epoch bias and the other switched components scored a little worse than the simpler model with a constant bias (CRPS10 of 0.891 against 0.850 on the eve of the 2024 election, and 1.562 against 1.351 at 180 days before the 2021 election; lower is better). The scoring records give no noise figure for this pair of models. For the simpler model against the 2024 model, the noise from the election's outcome alone is 0.06 and 0.21 points on these two cells, about the size of the differences here, so they are not evidence that either model forecasts better; the simpler one was kept, and on 10 Nov 2026 the epoch bias was removed with the file's other switches. The switched file, its harness and its step reports can be read at the tag v2-switched-final; the verdicts are in harness/v2/decisions.md, and the two sets of scores in harness/archive/v2/scoring.md and harness/v2/scoring.md.

The working v2 forecast keeps one constant bias, as the forecast model does, but with no hedge:

  • The industry bias $\mu_1$ is one sum_to_zero_vector[P] for the whole window, and each house's deviation $d_h$ is constant too.
  • A newcomer, from its entry epoch on, takes the mean of the existing parties' biases plus $s_{\text{new}}\eta$, with $s_{\text{new}} = 0.2023$ fixed.
  • At a result the bias takes no step. Instead the latent walk moves faster on the step leaving each closed election: that step is scaled by the campaign multiplier, so post-election movement can go to the walk rather than to the bias.
  • Fylgisvakt v5 still has the epoch bias.

What is open

Open
  • The open epoch is measured at the wrong end. Not yet backtested. The test: fit to about 60 days after each election and compare that open-epoch bias with the closed-epoch estimate the next result gives.
  • Alternatives to one logit-scale $\tau$: a per-party scale, a share-scaled scale, or a mean-reverting bias with an AR(1) pull towards each party's long-run bias. The dossier's $\lambda = \tau^2/(\tau^2+\omega^2/E)$ is the shrinkage form; a random walk carries the last epoch forward in full. None has been built or tested.
  • $\tau$ ignores the time between elections. v5's bias walk takes the same step across a one-year and a four-year epoch.
  • The $\tau_\mu$ prior. Its posterior median sits at the half-N(0, 0.1) prior's 86th percentile (90% interval: 74th–94th): the data pull it up, though not into the tail. Whether to widen it is undecided.
  • The forecast model's issues: the Annað reference and J closing; the hedge's unrecorded intent, broken zero sum and 2024-only divisors; the recycled anchor totals still in data files written before PR #15.
  • v2's open choices: Prósent as MMR's continuation (spec §12 Q2); splitting Björt framtíð out of Annað (Q3).
  • Stan/polling_watch_variants.md §10. It reads the logit sign as direction: by the pp method, E5's industry bias overstates S, by +1.31 pp (90% −1.55 to 4.24), averaged over E5's polls. Its backtests ran v5 before the 5 Nov entry rules and have not been re-run (§11).
